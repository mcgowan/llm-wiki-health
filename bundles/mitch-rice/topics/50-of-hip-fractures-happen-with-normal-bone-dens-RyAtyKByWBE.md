# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice contends that standard DEXA bone-density scans measure only the mineral ("concrete") component of bone and miss the collagen protein scaffolding ("rebar") that gives bone its flexibility, which he says is why roughly half of fragility hip fractures occur in women whose DEXA scans showed no osteoporosis[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE]. He attributes accelerating post-50 bone fragility to three compounding factors: declining estrogen and testosterone (with postmenopausal women said to lose 1.5-2.5% of bone mass yearly for a decade), reduced stomach-acid-dependent calcium absorption, and loss of the muscle mass that mechanically stresses bone and signals it to stay strong[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE].

# The "calcium trap" and hidden culprits

Rice describes a "calcium trap": calcium taken without vitamin K2 can deposit in arteries and joints instead of bone, since D3 aids calcium absorption while K2 directs it into bone; he cites a 3-year randomized trial in which postmenopausal women taking 180 mcg of MK-7 vitamin K2 daily had reduced spine and hip bone loss compared with placebo[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE]. He also flags long-term acid-blocker use (omeprazole and similar PPIs) as raising hip and spine fracture risk by a cited 20-44%, and notes that most older adults under-eat protein relative to bone needs, referencing a meta-analysis that found people over 65 eating above the standard 0.8 g/kg protein recommendation had an 11% lower hip fracture risk[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE].

# The three-layer protocol

- Layer 1 (foundation): nightly magnesium glycinate (~480 mg), vitamin D3 (2,000-5,000 IU), and vitamin K2/MK-7 (180-360 mcg), with at least 1,200 mg/day of calcium prioritized from food (Greek yogurt, sardines, leafy greens) and no more than 500 mg per supplemental dose[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE].
- Layer 2 ("load the bones"): resistance training at least twice weekly plus daily impact loading such as 10-20 light jumps or hops, since he says bones only rebuild in response to mechanical stress; he cites a 2025 meta-analysis reporting resistance training increased hip and spine bone density in postmenopausal women within a few months[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE].
- Layer 3 ("eat like a builder"): roughly 1-1.5 g of protein per kilogram of body weight daily (about 40 g per meal) from eggs, fish, poultry, Greek yogurt, lean red meat, and lentils[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE].

# Caveats

The video synthesizes several studies described only vaguely, without authors, journals, or sample sizes for most claims, and promotes Rice's own affiliate-linked magnesium, vitamin D3/K2, and calcium supplements as well as his free "metabolic blueprint" opt-in resource[^50-of-hip-fractures-happen-with-normal-bone-dens-RyAtyKByWBE].
