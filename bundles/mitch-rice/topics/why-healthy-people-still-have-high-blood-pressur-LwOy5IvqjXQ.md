# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that people who eat well, exercise, avoid smoking, and limit salt can still have persistently high blood pressure because control of blood pressure is not driven by lifestyle habits alone but by a hormonal signal — chronically elevated insulin.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ] He contends that for many adults, especially those over 60, blood pressure is less a "salt problem" than an insulin problem: rising insulin tells the kidneys to retain sodium, keeps the nervous system mildly activated, and keeps blood vessels from fully relaxing.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ] When meals and snacks are spaced too close together, insulin never falls back to a low baseline between eating events, so these three effects can persist around the clock even when the food choices themselves are healthy.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ]

# Mechanism and common patterns

He uses the analogy of the kidneys as sodium "exit ramps" that insulin keeps partly closed; cutting salt reduces how much sodium enters the system but does not reopen the exits if insulin stays elevated, which he offers as the reason some people cut sodium aggressively yet see little change in their readings.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ] Three everyday patterns are named as the main drivers of chronically elevated insulin: grazing on small meals or snacks every two to three hours, drinking liquid calories such as smoothies, juice, or protein shakes that spike insulin quickly without producing much fullness, and eating close to bedtime, which keeps insulin elevated overnight and is linked to higher morning blood pressure readings.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ] He also notes that visceral fat — fat packed around internal organs rather than under the skin — can keep insulin elevated even in people of normal weight, since it interferes with insulin signaling and forces the pancreas to release more insulin to keep blood sugar normal; this is offered as an explanation for why lean, active-looking people can still have elevated blood pressure.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ]

# The protocol

The recommended fix is not a specific diet but building daily "insulin-low windows": stop snacking between meals, finish dinner earlier, skip late-night eating, and compress food intake into roughly a 10-to-12-hour window each day so insulin can fall to baseline for extended stretches.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ] He states that as insulin falls, sodium retention eases, the nervous system settles, and blood vessels relax, and that blood pressure improvements can appear within days to weeks of consistent practice.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-blood-pressur-LwOy5IvqjXQ]

# Caveats

This is a single YouTube video presenting one physician's clinical framework rather than a systematic review of evidence; no specific studies, sample sizes, or citations are given for the insulin–blood pressure mechanism or the claimed "days to weeks" improvement timeline.
