# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that standard blood panels are designed to detect disease that has already developed, not the slow metabolic processes that build toward disease over years[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY]. He presents six additional blood tests he says are rarely ordered at routine physicals but reveal early warning signs of cardiovascular, metabolic, kidney, and liver problems well before conventional labs flag anything[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY]. He singles out the last test on his list, fasting insulin, as the one he considers most important and most underused[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].

# The six tests

- **Uric acid**: beyond its association with gout, he states rising uric acid is an early sign of insulin resistance and metabolic stress; while labs flag anything under 7 as normal, he prefers levels below 5.5 for better blood pressure and insulin sensitivity outcomes[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].
- **Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)]**: a largely genetic, mostly diet/exercise-resistant cholesterol particle he says adheres to artery walls more aggressively than standard LDL and raises heart attack/stroke risk even with otherwise normal labs; he states roughly one in five people have an elevated, undiagnosed level, and that it only needs to be checked once in a lifetime[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].
- **ApoB (apolipoprotein B)**: a direct count of atherogenic cholesterol particles rather than an estimate of cholesterol weight (as with standard LDL); he says a person can have normal LDL yet an excess of small, dense, damaging particles, particularly with prediabetes or fatty liver[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].
- **High-sensitivity CRP (hs-CRP)**: measures chronic low-grade inflammation that stiffens arteries without symptoms; he defines below 1.0 as low risk, 1.0-3.0 as moderate, and above 3.0 as higher risk, and notes it responds quickly (within weeks) to better sleep, diet, weight loss, and stress reduction, as well as omega-3 supplementation[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].
- **Cystatin C**: a kidney-function marker he prefers alongside creatinine because creatinine depends on muscle mass and can falsely reassure people with low muscle (common over 60), whereas cystatin C catches early kidney decline sooner[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].
- **ALT (a liver enzyme)**: he notes the "normal" upper limit has drifted from roughly 25 decades ago to 40-50 today as population health has worsened, and that ALT climbing into the mid-20s alongside belly fat or fatigue can signal early, reversible fatty liver[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].
- **Fasting insulin**: framed as the earliest of all six signals, since insulin resistance can build for 10-15 years before blood glucose itself rises; he targets fasting insulin under 8, and ideally under 6 in higher-risk patients, noting it is often the first marker to improve with successful lifestyle changes[^these-6-blood-tests-will-probably-save-your-life-DM0NEWoLFAY].

# Caveats

None of the specific risk thresholds or prevalence figures (e.g., the "one in five" Lp(a) statistic, the historical ALT reference ranges) are attributed to a named study or citation — they are presented as the presenter's own clinical framework.
