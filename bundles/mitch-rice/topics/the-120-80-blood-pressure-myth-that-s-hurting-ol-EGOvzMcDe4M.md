# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that aggressively treating blood pressure down to the standard 120/80 target can cause net harm in many older adults, and that current guidelines already support higher, individualized targets for this age group.[^the-120-80-blood-pressure-myth-that-s-hurting-ol-EGOvzMcDe4M] He frames 120/80 as a reasonable target built mostly from research done in younger adults, and argues that the aging cardiovascular system — stiffer arteries, blunted blood-pressure reflexes, and altered diastolic filling — changes the risk-benefit calculation for people over 60.[^the-120-80-blood-pressure-myth-that-s-hurting-ol-EGOvzMcDe4M]

# Three reasons 120/80 can backfire in older adults

- Reduced organ perfusion: stiffened arteries need a higher driving pressure to adequately perfuse the brain and kidneys; pushing pressure down to 120/80 in someone with stiff arteries can leave those organs underperfused, producing dizziness, brain fog, or fatigue even though the numbers "look good" on paper.[^the-120-80-blood-pressure-myth-that-s-hurting-ol-EGOvzMcDe4M]
- Fall risk from orthostatic hypotension: overly aggressive treatment, especially with multiple medications, can cause blood pressure to drop too much on standing, since older adults with stiffer arteries compensate more slowly; Rice cites studies associating aggressive blood-pressure treatment with higher rates of falls, fractures, and hospitalization.[^the-120-80-blood-pressure-myth-that-s-hurting-ol-EGOvzMcDe4M]
- Widened pulse pressure and diastolic dysfunction: as arteries stiffen, systolic pressure tends to rise while diastolic pressure holds steady or falls, widening the gap between the two ("pulse pressure"). Driving systolic down to 120 can push diastolic below 60, and since the heart's own coronary arteries fill during diastole, a diastolic pressure that low can reduce blood flow to the heart muscle itself — potentially raising rather than lowering cardiac risk.[^the-120-80-blood-pressure-myth-that-s-hurting-ol-EGOvzMcDe4M]

# What target to use instead

Rather than a universal 120/80 goal, Rice cites guidance supporting a target of roughly 130-140 systolic for adults over 60, and as high as 140-150 for adults over 75 who are frail, fall-prone, or managing multiple chronic conditions. He emphasizes individualizing the target to how a patient actually feels (energy, dizziness, kidney function) rather than the number alone, and favors reaching a healthy range through lifestyle measures — potassium-rich foods, isometric exercise, sleep, and stress management — before layering on medication.[^the-120-80-blood-pressure-myth-that-s-hurting-ol-EGOvzMcDe4M]

# Caveats

The specific fall/fracture/hospitalization studies and the pulse-pressure/diastolic-dysfunction claims are referenced only in general terms ("studies have shown," "current evidence including major guidelines") without named sources, so the strength and applicability of that evidence cannot be checked here.
