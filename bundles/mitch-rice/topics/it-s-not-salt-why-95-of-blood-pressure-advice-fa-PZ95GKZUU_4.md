# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice's central claim is that standard "cut your sodium" advice is incomplete because blood pressure responds to the balance between sodium and potassium, not sodium in isolation, and that focusing on sodium alone leaves several other drivers of high blood pressure unaddressed[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4]. He states the average American eats about 3,400 mg of sodium and only ~2,600 mg of potassium daily, well below the 3,500-4,700 mg recommended, and argues that sodium restriction without correcting this ratio provides limited benefit[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].

# Additional drivers beyond sodium

- **Hidden sugar in "heart-healthy" foods** — flavored yogurts, granola bars, fruit juice, cereals, sports drinks, and bottled dressings often carry high sugar despite health marketing; sugar (particularly fructose) raises uric acid, which signals the kidneys to retain sodium regardless of dietary sodium intake[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].
- **Blood vessel stiffness from aging**, which raises pressure independent of sodium and is addressed through movement and diet, not salt restriction[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].
- **Insulin resistance** — chronically elevated insulin (from cells not responding properly) tells the kidneys to retain sodium and tightens blood vessels, similar to the uric acid pathway[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].
- **Chronic nervous system overactivation** — ongoing stress keeps the body in "fight or flight," raising heart rate and vessel tightness regardless of diet[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].

# Recommended fixes

- **Correct the sodium-to-potassium ratio** rather than only cutting sodium: he cites potassium intake as capable of lowering systolic pressure 5-8 points on its own, from sources like beans, lentils, avocados, sweet potatoes, leafy greens, bananas, plain yogurt, and salmon, alongside a sodium target of roughly 1,500-2,300 mg/day[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].
- **Remove hidden sugar** by swapping flavored yogurts, juices, cereals, and granola bars for plain yogurt, whole fruit, eggs, nuts, and whole grains without added sugar[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].
- **Target insulin resistance** via eating protein before carbs, a 10-minute walk after meals, and swapping refined carbs for beans, steel-cut oats, and sweet potatoes[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].
- **Correct nervous system tone** via slow breathing (4 seconds in, 6-8 seconds out), consistent sleep, and isometric hand-grip training (30-45 second squeezes, 3-4 reps), the last cited as lowering systolic pressure 7-10 points over a few weeks[^it-s-not-salt-why-95-of-blood-pressure-advice-fa-PZ95GKZUU_4].

# Caveats

The claim that "sodium-to-potassium ratio is a stronger predictor of heart risk than sodium intake alone" and the specific point-drop figures for potassium and isometric training are stated as "studies show" without naming any specific study, sample size, or publication. The video promotes a free "blood pressure blueprint" guide as a lead magnet.
