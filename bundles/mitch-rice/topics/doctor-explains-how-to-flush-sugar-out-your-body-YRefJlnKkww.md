# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice claims blood sugar isn't only shaped by daytime food choices, but that a specific overnight window offers an especially efficient opportunity for the body to clear excess glucose, and that most people fail to use it, potentially dropping blood sugar 30-50 points overnight if used correctly[^doctor-explains-how-to-flush-sugar-out-your-body-YRefJlnKkww]. He presents four practical evening tactics, each tied to a distinct physiological mechanism, intended to reduce the glucose load the liver has to manage overnight and to support the kidneys' capacity to clear excess sugar[^doctor-explains-how-to-flush-sugar-out-your-body-YRefJlnKkww].

# The four tactics

- **A 10-minute walk 20-30 minutes after the last meal.** Muscle contraction during this window pulls glucose into muscle cells via GLUT4 transporters independent of insulin, blunting the post-meal glucose spike and giving the liver a calmer glucose load as it replenishes glycogen overnight. Rice also advises shifting carbohydrate-heavy foods to lunch rather than dinner, since dinner is typically followed by less movement[^doctor-explains-how-to-flush-sugar-out-your-body-YRefJlnKkww].
- **Sleeping in a cooler room (66-68°F rather than the mid-70s).** He states this modestly increases brown adipose tissue activity, which burns glucose for heat production, and cites research associating cooler sleep temperatures with improved overnight glucose metabolism and better next-morning insulin sensitivity — a passive change requiring only a lower thermostat setting[^doctor-explains-how-to-flush-sugar-out-your-body-YRefJlnKkww].
- **Front-loading hydration earlier in the day, tapering off about two hours before bed.** Since the kidneys eliminate excess glucose through urine but are limited by osmotic capacity, dehydration is said to physically limit how much glucose can be cleared; drinking most fluids from waking until mid-afternoon avoids the sleep disruption caused by nighttime bathroom trips[^doctor-explains-how-to-flush-sugar-out-your-body-YRefJlnKkww].
- **Diluted apple cider vinegar (1-2 tablespoons in 8 oz water) shortly before the last meal or bed.** Rice says controlled trials show this lowers next-morning fasting glucose via two mechanisms: slowed gastric emptying (blunting how fast glucose enters the bloodstream) and improved cellular insulin sensitivity. He cautions to dilute it, since undiluted vinegar can damage teeth and irritate the esophagus, and notes capsule forms are an alternative[^doctor-explains-how-to-flush-sugar-out-your-body-YRefJlnKkww].

# Note on medication

Rice acknowledges that SGLT2 inhibitor medications (e.g., Jardiance, Farxiga, Invokana) work by a legitimate, related mechanism — forcing the kidneys to excrete excess glucose in urine — and says they are necessary and appropriate for many patients, but argues they shouldn't be a substitute for the lifestyle tactics described, which should be tried alongside medication where relevant rather than relied on as a replacement for it[^doctor-explains-how-to-flush-sugar-out-your-body-YRefJlnKkww].

# Caveats

The specific numeric claim of a 30-50 point overnight blood sugar drop is not tied to any cited study, sample size, or specific population, nor are the referenced "research" and "multiple controlled trials" on room temperature and apple cider vinegar named or sourced in the video. The video includes affiliate Amazon links for specific apple cider vinegar liquid and capsule products, and promotes his free "Metabolic Blueprint" resource — commercial elements alongside the medical content. People on diabetes medication should not change their regimen based on this video without consulting their prescribing physician.
