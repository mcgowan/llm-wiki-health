# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that the widely used target of 120/80 mmHg was calibrated on younger populations and can be inappropriate — even dangerous — for older adults, because a "normal" reading pursued too aggressively can cause more harm than a moderately elevated one.[^your-blood-pressure-target-was-designed-for-a-40-i7Wu3Z1Pbl8] He explains that arteries stiffen with age, which raises baseline blood pressure but also impairs the body's ability to adjust pressure quickly, for instance when standing up, becoming dehydrated, or overheating — a phenomenon called orthostatic hypotension that can cause brief dizziness in a 40-year-old but a fall, hip fracture, or loss of independence in a person in their 70s or 80s.[^your-blood-pressure-target-was-designed-for-a-40-i7Wu3Z1Pbl8]

# Individualizing the target

Using two hypothetical 75-year-old patients with identical readings of 142/92, he illustrates that an active patient tolerating medication without symptoms benefits from treatment toward a lower target, while a frailer patient with balance problems and dizziness on standing could be harmed by pushing toward 120/80, since further blood pressure reduction can outpace the aging regulatory system's ability to keep blood flowing to the brain on standing.[^your-blood-pressure-target-was-designed-for-a-40-i7Wu3Z1Pbl8] He notes that frail, symptomatic patients like the second case are often excluded from the clinical trials that inform standard guidelines, which he argues undermines a one-size-fits-all target for everyone over 60.[^your-blood-pressure-target-was-designed-for-a-40-i7Wu3Z1Pbl8] His general guidance: adults over 60 who are active and tolerate medication well can reasonably aim for a systolic reading below 140, sometimes closer to 130; but anyone experiencing dizziness, weakness, falls, or a big drop on standing should not be pushed toward the lower target, since symptoms should outweigh the number.[^your-blood-pressure-target-was-designed-for-a-40-i7Wu3Z1Pbl8]

# Self-testing for orthostatic hypotension

He describes a simple home test: measure blood pressure seated, wait two minutes, stand, and re-measure within one minute; a systolic drop greater than 20 mmHg or diastolic drop greater than 10 mmHg accompanied by lightheadedness indicates orthostatic hypotension and should be reported to a physician.[^your-blood-pressure-target-was-designed-for-a-40-i7Wu3Z1Pbl8] He is careful to state that untreated hypertension still carries real long-term risks (stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney disease), and that the point is balancing that risk against symptoms, not dismissing high blood pressure as harmless.[^your-blood-pressure-target-was-designed-for-a-40-i7Wu3Z1Pbl8]

# Caveats

The two-patient comparison is a hypothetical illustration rather than a cited case series, and no clinical trial or guideline document is named for the specific numeric targets given. The video promotes affiliate-linked supplements described as lowering blood pressure "as much as prescription medications" in combination, plus a free lead-magnet guide ("Blood Pressure Blueprint") — both claims warrant skepticism absent supporting data.
