# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice's core claim is that "too high" for blood pressure cannot be judged from a single reading, and that many older adults are either dismissed as "normal for your age" or placed on unnecessary medication because clinicians (and patients) act on unrepresentative, non-resting numbers[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs]. He frames systolic pressure (the peak pressure during a heartbeat) and diastolic pressure (the background pressure between beats) as reflecting different physiology — systolic naturally drifts upward with age-related artery stiffening, while diastolic reflects more constant vascular tension — and argues both should be tracked, but neither should be acted on from a single, non-resting measurement[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs].

His central reasoning is that blood pressure is a highly dynamic value that can swing 20–40 points across a day in response to movement, stress, caffeine, pain, or temperature, so a single elevated reading is "reactive," not a true baseline, and only a pattern of resting, averaged readings over weeks reveals genuine risk[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs].

# Thresholds and measurement protocol

- **Systolic ranges he cites**: below 120 optimal, 120–129 acceptable, 130–139 often reasonable in older adults who feel well, 140–149 warrants attention (not automatically medication), and 150+ at rest as the level he treats seriously[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs].
- **Diastolic ranges he cites**: below 80 ideal, 80–85 usually fine, 85–89 worth watching over time, and 90+ at rest as deserving attention[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs].
- **His overall cutoff**: consistently ≥150 systolic and ≥90 diastolic at rest is "too high" for most people over 60; for people in their 20s–40s, he lowers this to above 140 systolic or above 85 diastolic[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs].
- **Home measurement checklist**: sit quietly 5 minutes with back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level, no talking, empty bladder; take two readings one minute apart and record the lower one; do this about 20 minutes after waking and again before bed; alternate arms daily; average 14 readings over a week to establish a true baseline[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs].
- **Added red flags** that lower his threshold for concern regardless of the numbers: blood pressure that doesn't recover after exercise, existing kidney disease, prior stroke or heart disease, or symptoms like chest pain, shortness of breath, or visual changes[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-pressure-BUB0qUjA1vs].

# Caveats

This is a single practitioner's clinical framework and self-reported thresholds, not an official guideline; the specific cutoffs (e.g., 150/90 for over-60s) differ from major society guidelines and are presented without citation to a specific study or consensus statement.
