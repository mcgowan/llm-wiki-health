# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice contends that five under-ordered health numbers predict long-term cardiovascular, metabolic, and cognitive health more reliably than the commonly tracked trio of weight, A1C, and office blood pressure.[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98] He argues these markers do not simply add risk together but compound it, citing a claimed meta-analysis in which having three or more elevated markers raised cardiovascular and cognitive disease risk up to tenfold rather than threefold, and singles out fasting insulin as the root cause feeding into the other four.[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98]

# The five markers

- **Fasting insulin**: framed as an early-warning marker that can be elevated for 10–15 years before fasting glucose or A1C shift, since a struggling pancreas compensates by overproducing insulin. His target range is 6–8 (versus typical lab flags only above 25).[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98]
- **ApoB**: said to count atherogenic lipid particles directly (each LDL, VLDL, and IDL particle carries one ApoB molecule), unlike LDL, which measures cholesterol mass and can look normal while particle count is high. Targets cited: below 80 for higher-risk patients, below 100 for lower-risk adults.[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98]
- **Waist-to-height ratio**: used as a free proxy for visceral fat, which is described as functioning like an inflammatory organ that drives insulin resistance and blood pressure independent of overall body weight. Rule of thumb: waist circumference under half of height.[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98]
- **Home/ambulatory blood pressure**: presented as more reliable than a single office reading, since office measurements are often taken incorrectly; he recommends regular home monitoring to establish a true baseline.[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98]
- **hs-CRP**: a marker of chronic low-grade inflammation linked to joint aches, brain fog, and poor recovery, with risk zones given as under 1.0 (low), 1.0–3.0 (moderate), and above 3.0 (high). Described as responsive to better sleep, less refined carbohydrate intake, movement, and stress reduction within weeks, with fish oil supplementation also mentioned as supportive.[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98]

# Why fasting insulin is emphasized

Rice describes a cascade in which chronically elevated insulin drives visceral fat accumulation, which raises hs-CRP, which stiffens arteries and raises blood pressure, which in turn accelerates ApoB-related arterial damage — presenting fasting insulin testing as the single highest-leverage addition to a standard checkup.[^5-life-saving-tests-your-doctor-might-skip-zmU56dUWq98]

# Caveats

The specific studies referenced (Journal of the American College of Cardiology on ApoB and waist-to-height ratio, New England Journal of Medicine on waist circumference and on hs-CRP, Journal of Neurology on hs-CRP and cognition, and an unnamed meta-analysis on combined risk markers) are named only by publication, without authors, sample sizes, or links, so their precise findings cannot be verified here. The video also promotes an Amazon affiliate link for a fish oil supplement and a waitlist for the presenter's own paid health community.
