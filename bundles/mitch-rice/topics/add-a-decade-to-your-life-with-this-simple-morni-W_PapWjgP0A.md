# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents a six-step morning routine he says supports a healthy cortisol awakening response and, over time, slows biological aging, arguing that the first hour after waking sets cortisol, blood sugar, metabolic rate, and inflammation patterns for the rest of the day[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A]. His core mechanism is that a properly timed morning cortisol spike (peaking 30-45 minutes after waking) is protective, while a blunted, chronically flattened cortisol pattern — caused by stress, rushing, or immediate phone use — drives visceral fat gain, cellular aging, and cardiovascular risk[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A].

# The six steps

- Avoid checking the phone for the first 20 minutes after waking, since he argues reactive scrolling activates the amygdala and prematurely spikes cortisol; he cites a Journal of Experimental Psychology study linking morning smartphone use to increased stress and cognitive fragmentation[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A].
- Get outside for 5-10 minutes within the first 30 minutes of waking, with direct (non-sunglassed) light exposure to anchor circadian rhythm; ashwagandha is suggested as an optional supplement to further lower cortisol[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A].
- Drink about 500 mL (16 oz) of water before coffee or food, since overnight fluid loss is said to mildly dehydrate most people and elevate cortisol; a pinch of sea salt or a slice of lemon is suggested for electrolytes[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A].
- Move the body for 10 minutes (a brisk walk or light resistance work), said to trigger BDNF release, clear residual blood glucose, and activate lymphatic drainage[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A].
- Eat at least 30 g of protein within 60 minutes of waking (eggs, Greek yogurt, or a protein shake) to reduce cortisol reactivity and support muscle protein synthesis, described as increasingly important after age 40[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A].
- Spend 5 minutes in stillness (prayer, reflection, or slow breathing), said to lower cortisol, reduce inflammatory markers, and support telomere length[^add-a-decade-to-your-life-with-this-simple-morni-W_PapWjgP0A].
