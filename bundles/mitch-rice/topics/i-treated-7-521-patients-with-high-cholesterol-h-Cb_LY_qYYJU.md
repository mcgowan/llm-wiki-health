# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice's central claim is that for most patients with elevated cholesterol, the primary driver is not dietary saturated fat but insulin resistance in the liver, and that diet changes like cutting eggs, red meat, and fat often fail to lower cholesterol because they don't address this underlying mechanism[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU]. He frames total cholesterol as a calculated sum (LDL + HDL + triglycerides/5) that can mask very different underlying risk profiles between two people with the same number, and argues LDL, HDL, triglycerides, and their ratios matter more than the total[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU].

His proposed mechanism: when the liver becomes insulin resistant (typically from diets high in refined carbohydrates and sugar), it converts excess glucose to fat via "de novo lipogenesis," packages that fat into VLDL particles that circulate and convert into LDL, and simultaneously reduces the activity of LDL receptors that normally clear LDL from the blood — producing more LDL production and less LDL clearance at once[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU].

# ApoB as a more informative marker

He argues that apolipoprotein B (apoB), which counts the actual number of cholesterol-carrying particles rather than the total cholesterol they carry, is a more predictive marker for arterial plaque risk than LDL alone, especially in people with insulin resistance where LDL particles become smaller and more numerous while the standard LDL number looks unremarkable[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU]. He recommends patients with insulin resistance, elevated triglycerides, or metabolic syndrome ask their doctor for an apoB test, calling it underused in routine care[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU].

# Recommended fixes

- **Reduce refined carbohydrates and added sugar** — described as the biggest lever, since these directly fuel de novo lipogenesis in the liver[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU].
- **Walk 10 minutes after meals** so muscle uptake reduces the glucose load reaching the liver[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU].
- **Build and maintain muscle** via 2-3 resistance training sessions weekly, since more muscle mass improves post-meal glucose clearance[^i-treated-7-521-patients-with-high-cholesterol-h-Cb_LY_qYYJU].

# Caveats

The "7,521 patients" figure is a self-reported clinical tally rather than data from a study, and no specific studies, sample sizes, or citations are given for the liver/insulin-cholesterol mechanism described. The video promotes a free "metabolic blueprint" guide as a lead magnet.
