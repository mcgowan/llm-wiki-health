# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice pushes back on the common advice given to older adults that exercise can no longer meaningfully change their blood pressure, arguing instead that blood pressure can still fall naturally after age 60 with the right type of training rather than more of the same walking.[^older-adults-lower-blood-pressure-naturally-with-1h8lZNn5dEc] He presents three low-impact isometric exercises — sustained muscle contractions without movement — that he says he has used with older patients to lower blood pressure without adding medication, plus a bonus movement practice and breathing technique to reinforce the effect.[^older-adults-lower-blood-pressure-naturally-with-1h8lZNn5dEc]

# The three exercises

- **Handgrip isometrics.** Squeezing a hand grip device, stress ball, or rolled towel at roughly 50% max effort is described as sending signals through the forearm muscles that lower baseline stress hormone activity and improve blood vessel function. He cites study evidence of a 5-10 point systolic drop from consistent handgrip training, comparable to some medications, using a protocol of 20-30 second holds, 30 seconds rest, four rounds per hand (about 4 minutes total); a softer object and shorter 10-15 second holds are suggested for those with arthritis.[^older-adults-lower-blood-pressure-naturally-with-1h8lZNn5dEc]
- **Wall sits.** Holding a partial squat against a wall is said to prompt leg muscles to release nitric oxide, improving arterial flexibility and lowering peripheral resistance. The standard version holds 20-30 seconds with 30 seconds rest, building toward 1-2 total minutes; a modified higher-angle version with hand support is offered for those with joint limitations.[^older-adults-lower-blood-pressure-naturally-with-1h8lZNn5dEc]
- **Planks (modified).** Planks are said to activate core muscles, improve posture and breathing mechanics, and support fall prevention. Modified entry points include wall planks and elevated planks on a counter, progressing to a standard plank on knees or toes, held 15-30 seconds and built up to 2-3 total minutes.[^older-adults-lower-blood-pressure-naturally-with-1h8lZNn5dEc]

# Bonus practices

Tai chi — slow rhythmic movement, weight shifting, and coordinated breathing — is offered as a bonus movement said to lower stress hormones and resting heart rate with as little as 5-10 minutes daily. "Resonance breathing" (inhaling through the nose for 5 seconds, exhaling through the mouth for 5 seconds, roughly six breaths per minute for 3-5 minutes) is recommended alongside all three exercises to further activate the vagus nerve.[^older-adults-lower-blood-pressure-naturally-with-1h8lZNn5dEc] He gives explicit safety cutoffs throughout — no sharp pain, numbness, chest tightness, or dizziness — and frames consistency and gentle depth as more important than intensity.[^older-adults-lower-blood-pressure-naturally-with-1h8lZNn5dEc]

# Caveats

The claimed effect sizes (5-10 point systolic drops) are described as coming from "multiple studies" without naming them, sample sizes, or populations studied. The video promotes the creator's paid "blood pressure reset guide" and includes an affiliate Amazon link for a yoga mat.
