# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice identifies drinking alcohol in the evening as a widespread sleep mistake that he says disrupts deep sleep enough to raise both dementia risk and blood pressure. He cites a Nature Communications study reporting a 30% higher dementia risk in midlife adults who consistently made this mistake, independent of other lifestyle factors, and a Journal of Hypertension study linking the same habit to elevated morning blood pressure even when daytime readings looked normal.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA] His underlying mechanism is that deep sleep drives two protective processes simultaneously — the brain's glymphatic waste-clearance system, which clears amyloid beta and tau (the proteins implicated in Alzheimer's disease), and "nocturnal dipping," a normal 10-20% overnight drop in blood pressure that gives arteries and the heart a recovery window — and that disrupting deep sleep blunts both at once.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA]

# Why alcohol is the culprit

Although alcohol is a sedative that can speed sleep onset, he argues falling asleep fast is not the same as sleeping well: alcohol sedates brain activity during the first half of the night rather than allowing normal deep-sleep cycling, then produces a rebound of fragmented, shallow sleep as it clears in the second half.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA] He cites a JMIR Mental Health study finding that even one to two evening drinks reduced deep sleep by up to 24%, which he says doubles lifetime dementia risk if repeated nightly, and an American Journal of Hypertension study finding evening alcohol directly prevents the nocturnal blood pressure dip.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA] He recommends cutting off alcohol at least 3 hours before bed as the single highest-leverage change.

# Other compounding factors

- **Late-night eating** within 2-3 hours of bed keeps insulin elevated overnight, suppressing the growth-hormone pulse tied to deep sleep and glymphatic function, and keeping the sympathetic nervous system partly activated.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA]
- **Screen light before bed** blocks melatonin, which he says plays a direct role in initiating deep sleep and regulating overnight blood pressure.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA]
- **Inconsistent sleep/wake timing** disrupts the circadian rhythm that governs when deep sleep and blood pressure dipping occur.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA]
- **Undiagnosed sleep apnea** causes repeated brief awakenings and blood pressure spikes — potentially hundreds per night — and is described as a leading cause of medication-resistant high blood pressure; he recommends a sleep study for people who snore, wake unrefreshed, or have blood pressure that resists treatment.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA]

He also recommends magnesium glycinate 30-60 minutes before bed as a way to support deep sleep quality.[^the-1-worst-sleep-mistake-destroying-your-brain-v37s1DBl4SA]
