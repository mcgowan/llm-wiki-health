# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice lays out what he presents as the three foundational strategies for lowering blood pressure naturally in adults over 60 — correcting dietary potassium, adding isometric exercise, and calming the nervous system through breathing — followed by five supplements he uses to reinforce those changes.[^the-best-way-for-older-adults-to-lower-blood-pre-4arhctIIaSM] His framing throughout is that blood pressure is less a "salt problem" and more a sodium-to-potassium balance and nervous-system-tone problem, and that consistent small changes matter more than any single dramatic intervention.[^the-best-way-for-older-adults-to-lower-blood-pre-4arhctIIaSM]

# Three core strategies

- Increase potassium intake (beans, lentils, avocados, sweet potatoes, yogurt, spinach, salmon, bananas): potassium is said to prompt the kidneys to excrete excess sodium and relax blood vessels, with claimed reductions of about 5-8 points in systolic pressure from dietary increases alone.[^the-best-way-for-older-adults-to-lower-blood-pre-4arhctIIaSM]
- Isometric exercise: wall sits, planks, or — as an easier substitute — squeezing a towel or hand-grip trainer for 30-45 seconds per hand, five repetitions, four days a week. This is said to outperform cardio for blood pressure specifically, lowering systolic pressure by roughly 7-10 points by training blood vessels to relax under sustained tension.[^the-best-way-for-older-adults-to-lower-blood-pre-4arhctIIaSM]
- Nervous-system calming via breathing: "resonance breathing" (inhale through the nose for 5 seconds, exhale through the mouth for 5 seconds, six breaths per minute for about 5 minutes) is described as lowering systolic pressure by 5-10 points by slowing heart rate and improving baroreceptor sensitivity; a "4-7-8" pattern (4-second inhale, 7-second hold, 8-second exhale) is recommended as a nighttime variant.[^the-best-way-for-older-adults-to-lower-blood-pre-4arhctIIaSM]

# Supporting supplements

Rice discusses five supplements as adjuncts, not replacements, for the strategies above: magnesium glycinate (said to relax vessels and calm the nervous system, with a claimed ~5-point systolic reduction), omega-3/fish oil (artery flexibility and inflammation), vitamin D (vascular and hormonal support, especially if deficient), coenzyme Q10 (supports vessel function, particularly relevant since some blood pressure medications lower natural CoQ10 levels), and potassium supplementation (used cautiously, food first, and avoided in kidney disease).[^the-best-way-for-older-adults-to-lower-blood-pre-4arhctIIaSM]

# Caveats

The specific point-drop figures for potassium, isometric exercise, breathing, and magnesium are presented as general findings ("studies show") without named sources or sample sizes.
