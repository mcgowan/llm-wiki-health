# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice outlines seven common pre-test behaviors he says commonly distort blood test results, arguing that patients and even some clinicians misinterpret abnormal values without accounting for these confounders[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg]. The overarching theme is that timing, hydration, exercise, medication disclosure, alcohol, and unmeasured environmental exposures can each shift specific lab markers independent of a person's actual baseline health[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].

# The seven mistakes

- Incorrect fasting: eating before a fasting lipid panel causes postprandial lipemia, inflating triglycerides and, through the calculation formula, LDL; he recommends an 8-12 hour fasting window (water is fine and even encouraged) for lipid and iron panels[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].
- Exercising intensely within 24 hours of a blood draw, which can transiently raise liver enzymes, white blood cell counts, and LDL, risking false positives on cardiac or muscle-injury workups[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].
- Testing at the wrong time of day: markers like cortisol (highest 6-8 a.m., lowest near midnight), iron, glucose, and testosterone follow circadian rhythms, so afternoon draws can look artificially abnormal[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].
- Dehydration, which concentrates blood plasma (hemoconcentration) and can falsely elevate BUN, creatinine, hemoglobin/hematocrit, and electrolyte readings; he recommends 8-16 oz (roughly 250-500 mL) of water before the draw[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].
- Not disclosing all medications and supplements — biotin is highlighted as notorious for skewing hormone and cardiac marker assays, and thyroid drugs, cholesterol drugs, GLP-1 medications, and exogenous testosterone can also distort results[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].
- Drinking alcohol the night before a test that includes liver enzymes; alcohol is mostly metabolized within about 12 hours, but he advises a 24-hour abstention window before liver panels specifically[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].
- Never checking for heavy-metal or environmental-toxin accumulation, which standard panels do not screen for, though he cites research linking elevated lead exposure to disrupted red blood cell production and elevated mercury exposure to raised hs-CRP inflammation[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].

# Caveats

None of the studies referenced include authors, sample sizes, or publication years; the heavy-metal claims are attributed only to unspecified "research," and the video promotes Rice's own paid private membership community for further blood-work interpretation[^7-hidden-mistakes-92-of-patients-make-before-blo-LZkmwecO5dg].
