# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents the "soleus pump" — a seated calf-raise exercise targeting the soleus muscle in the lower leg — as a fast way to lower post-meal blood sugar[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE]. He claims the soleus muscle is unusual because, unlike most muscles that burn their own stored glycogen during light activity, it draws glucose continuously and directly from the bloodstream due to its fiber type and high blood supply[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE]. Performed right after a meal, when blood glucose is flooding in, this is said to reduce the amount of insulin needed to clear that glucose, which over time is framed as improving insulin sensitivity and lowering the risk of prediabetes and type 2 diabetes[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE].

# The protocol

- **Soleus pump**: sit with heels planted on the floor, lift the toes and front of the foot upward, then lower repeatedly at a steady pace for about 10 minutes after eating — no standing, equipment, or leaving the chair required[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE].
- **Optional add-on**: a 10-minute brisk walk immediately after the soleus pump, timed to the 15-30 minute post-meal window when glucose is entering the bloodstream fastest, so leg muscles (including the soleus) pull glucose out at the same rate it is arriving[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE].
- **Suggested routine**: after every meal (breakfast, lunch, dinner) for 7 days, do the 10-minute soleus pump followed by a 10-minute walk; if only one component is feasible, he says the soleus pump alone for 10 minutes after eating is enough to matter, especially sustained over about 30 days[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE].

# Mechanism

The claimed mechanism rests on the soleus muscle's distinct metabolic behavior: rather than depleting its own glycogen stores first, it is described as preferentially and continuously extracting glucose from circulating blood when activated, even in people with impaired insulin signaling[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE]. Lower post-meal glucose spikes are said to translate into lower insulin demand, and chronically lower insulin levels are presented as the pathway to improved insulin sensitivity[^10-minutes-after-eating-flushes-sugar-instantly-YYnK-MyPLsE].

# Caveats

The video references "real research" and a study protocol involving hours of soleus muscle activation, but names no specific study, sample size, or publication, and the daily/7-day/30-day routines described are the presenter's own extrapolations rather than tested protocols. The video also promotes the creator's own paid private health community via a description link, a commercial interest worth noting.
