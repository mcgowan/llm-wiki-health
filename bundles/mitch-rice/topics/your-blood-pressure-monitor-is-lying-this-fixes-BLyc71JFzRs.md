# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that home blood pressure monitors frequently produce inaccurate readings not because the devices are broken, but because of everyday measurement mistakes that most people never account for.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs] He walks through four specific error sources — wrong cuff size, wrong arm position, checking at the wrong time of day, and relying on a single reading — and presents a step-by-step at-home protocol intended to produce a reliable baseline instead of noisy, misleading numbers.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs]

# Sources of error

A cuff that is too small for the arm compresses harder to occlude the artery and can inflate the systolic reading by 10–20 points, while an oversized cuff produces artificially low readings; he advises measuring arm circumference at the mid-bicep and matching it to the range printed on the cuff.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs] An arm hanging below heart level adds hydrostatic pressure that can raise readings by 10 or more mmHg, while an arm raised above heart level lowers them artificially; the arm should instead rest on a flat surface at roughly heart height.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs] Because blood pressure fluctuates with activity, stress, and meals throughout the day, a midday reading taken after a stressful task or brisk walk can run 10–20 points higher than a resting reading, so he recommends consistent timing — ideally each morning before caffeine, after five minutes of quiet sitting.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs] Finally, because any single reading only captures one moment of natural fluctuation, he argues it should never be treated as diagnostic on its own.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs]

# The at-home checklist

His full protocol: avoid caffeine and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand, empty the bladder, sit in a chair with back support and feet flat, rest the bare arm on a table at heart height with the cuff about an inch above the elbow crease, sit quietly for five minutes with slow breathing, then take two readings one minute apart and record the lower (or the average) of the two.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs] He recommends doing this twice daily (on waking and before bed) for a full week — 14 readings total — and averaging the top and bottom numbers separately to establish a "true resting baseline" rather than reacting to any one reading.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-this-fixes-BLyc71JFzRs]
