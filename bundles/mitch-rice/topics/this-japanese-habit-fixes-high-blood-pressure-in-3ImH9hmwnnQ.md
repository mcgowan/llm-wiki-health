# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents the traditional Okinawan eating pattern as a fast-acting blood-pressure intervention, arguing that the usual Western focus on cutting sodium misses the point because Okinawans eat more sodium than the US low-sodium target yet have roughly half the US rate of high blood pressure[^this-japanese-habit-fixes-high-blood-pressure-in-3ImH9hmwnnQ]. He attributes this instead to the sodium-to-potassium ratio, a very high-potassium diet, and the practice of "hara hachi bu" — eating until about 80% full — which together are said to produce measurable blood pressure reductions within about 48 to 72 hours[^this-japanese-habit-fixes-high-blood-pressure-in-3ImH9hmwnnQ].

# Three claimed mechanisms

- **Sodium-to-potassium balance**: sodium promotes fluid retention and tightens arterial smooth muscle, while potassium prompts the kidneys to excrete sodium and relaxes that same smooth muscle; the Okinawan diet is rich in potassium sources like sweet potatoes, seaweed, and vegetables, and he cites research in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition finding that raising potassium intake while holding sodium steady lowered systolic blood pressure within 3-7 days[^this-japanese-habit-fixes-high-blood-pressure-in-3ImH9hmwnnQ].
- **Hara hachi bu (eating to 80% full)**: stopping before full reduces total calories and, he argues, blunts the size of the post-meal insulin spike; elevated insulin is said to independently raise blood pressure by increasing sodium and water retention in the kidneys[^this-japanese-habit-fixes-high-blood-pressure-in-3ImH9hmwnnQ].
- **Fiber and gut bacteria**: the same Okinawan foods (sweet potato, seaweed, vegetables, bitter melon) are high in fiber, which gut bacteria ferment into short-chain fatty acids that act on kidney and blood-vessel receptors independent of the sodium/potassium pathway; he states that meaningful shifts in gut bacterial composition and short-chain fatty acid production can occur within 48-72 hours of increased fiber intake, on a similar timescale to the kidney's response to potassium[^this-japanese-habit-fixes-high-blood-pressure-in-3ImH9hmwnnQ].

# The protocol

He recommends eating more slowly and deliberately stopping at roughly 80% fullness at every meal, while working toward 3,500-4,700 mg of potassium per day from foods such as baked white and sweet potatoes, avocados, bananas, wild-caught salmon, milk, Greek yogurt, and beans[^this-japanese-habit-fixes-high-blood-pressure-in-3ImH9hmwnnQ]. He also suggests magnesium glycinate as an additional supplement to support vascular relaxation alongside higher potassium intake[^this-japanese-habit-fixes-high-blood-pressure-in-3ImH9hmwnnQ].

# Caveats

The "72 hours" framing is an extrapolation built by combining separate findings (a 3-7 day potassium study and a 48-72 hour gut microbiome claim) rather than a single study testing the full Okinawan protocol as described; specific studies are named only loosely (journal name given, no authors, year, or sample size).
