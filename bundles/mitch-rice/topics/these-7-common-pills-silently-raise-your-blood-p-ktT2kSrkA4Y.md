# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice catalogues seven categories of common medications and supplements that raise blood pressure, most of them without the patient's awareness. Two mechanisms recur across the list: **vasoconstriction** (norepinephrine-driven or direct tightening of vessels) and **salt-and-water retention** (more fluid in the "pipes" means more pressure). His closing argument is clinical rather than pharmacological: many hypertension diagnoses are made during a window when the patient happens to be on one of these agents, and a busy clinician may prescribe a lifelong antihypertensive without asking what else the patient is taking.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]

# The seven classes

1. **SNRI antidepressants** (venlafaxine/Effexor, duloxetine/Cymbalta, desvenlafaxine/Pristiq). Rice cites ~14% of Americans on them. By raising norepinephrine — which constricts vessels body-wide, not just in the brain — they keep vessels slightly tighter all day. The effect is dose-dependent; venlafaxine shows a measurable rise above ~150 mg/day. SSRIs (Prozac, Zoloft, Lexapro) generally do not do this.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]
2. **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen/Advil/Motrin, naproxen/Aleve). Cause salt and water retention and mild vasoconstriction. Unlike SNRIs the effect is *not* dose-dependent, so even a routine 200 mg ibuprofen for headaches contributes.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]
3. **Decongestants** (pseudoephedrine, phenylephrine). They clear the nose by squeezing vessels, and that squeeze happens everywhere at once.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]
4. **Corticosteroids** (prednisone, methylprednisolone). Tell the kidneys to retain salt, and water follows; the effect builds with duration. Even a short Medrol dose pack for poison ivy can push pressure up.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]
5. **Estrogen-containing birth control and menopausal HRT.** Not every contraceptive, but estrogen-containing ones raise pressure in some people via salt/water retention.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]
6. **Stimulants** for ADHD and narcolepsy (Adderall, methylphenidate). Activate the sympathetic fight-or-flight system for as long as the drug is active. Rice checks heart rate and blood pressure in every patient on them.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]
7. **Herbal supplements**: ephedra (ma huang), high-dose ginseng, licorice root, and St. John's wort. Licorice contains a compound that tricks the kidneys into retaining salt via a steroid-like pathway. "Natural" does not mean safe at high doses or in combination, and some of these match prescription drugs in effect size.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]

# What to do

Rice explicitly warns against stopping any of these on your own — some are genuinely necessary and abrupt discontinuation can be as dangerous as the pressure rise. His prescribed steps: read every label, note anything in these seven categories, and ask a pharmacist or doctor two questions — is this absolutely necessary, and is there an alternative? Suggested swaps: acetaminophen (Tylenol) instead of NSAIDs for pain, and saline sprays or decongestant-free antihistamines for congestion.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]

# The misdiagnosis argument

The video's central claim is that a hypertension label is often treated as permanent when its origin was situational: a new SNRI, allergy-season decongestants, or daily ibuprofen for joint pain at the moment blood pressure was checked. Rice says he has taken patients off antihypertensives simply by identifying and replacing the offending agent, after which pressure normalized on its own. He urges viewers who recognise this pattern to raise it with their clinician, and promotes a downloadable guide on how to have that conversation inside his paid community.[^these-7-common-pills-silently-raise-your-blood-p-ktT2kSrkA4Y]
