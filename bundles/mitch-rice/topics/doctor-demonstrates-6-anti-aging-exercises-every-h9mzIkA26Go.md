# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents a six-exercise strength routine designed around what he says is the biggest fear he hears from patients over 60: losing independence through falls, inability to rise from the floor, or difficulty with stairs[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go]. The exercises target the muscle groups and movement patterns (squatting, stepping, hip extension, single-leg balance, pushing, and core bracing) he argues most directly protect against falls and preserve the ability to perform everyday tasks like climbing stairs, rising from a chair, or carrying groceries[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].

# The routine

A 3-5 minute warm-up (neck rotations, shoulder rolls, arm swings and torso twists, wrist rolls, ankle rolls, and leg swings) is recommended first, since joint fluid production slows and connective tissue stiffens with age, making an unwarmed joint more prone to strain[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go]. The six main exercises are:

1. **Squat** — feet shoulder-width apart, hips driving the movement rather than the knees tracking past the toes; trains glutes, hamstrings, and quadriceps used to rise from low surfaces. Two sets of 10; a chair can be held for support as a modification[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].
2. **Step-up** — stepping fully onto a stair or sturdy box one leg at a time, core engaged; trains the coordination and strength needed to navigate stairs safely, a common site of falls[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].
3. **Glute bridge** — lying on the back, hips raised and held for 3 seconds; targets glutes, which Rice says weaken early with age and contribute to back pain and falls. Two sets of 10, with a single-leg variation for progression[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].
4. **Lunge** — stepping forward and lowering both knees toward 90 degrees; trains single-leg balance and stability, described as one of the most studied predictors of fall risk. 10 repetitions per leg[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].
5. **Push-up** — trains chest, shoulders, triceps, and core; knee push-ups are offered as a beginner modification, with wide-hand and diamond variations for more advanced training[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].
6. **Plank** — held on forearms and toes in a straight line for 20-30 seconds, building toward longer holds, avoiding hips rising above body line[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].

A cooldown of quadriceps, hamstring/glute, and downward-dog stretches follows to close out the session[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].

# Safety guidance

Rice emphasizes prioritizing form and balance over speed or repetition count, avoiding overexertion when returning to exercise after a long break, never skipping warm-up or cooldown, and consulting a doctor or physical therapist if proper form can't be maintained on any movement[^doctor-demonstrates-6-anti-aging-exercises-every-h9mzIkA26Go].

# Caveats

This is a general demonstration routine from a single video, not an individualized exercise prescription, and no clinical studies are cited for the specific claims made (e.g., the degree to which these exercises reduce fall risk). The video promotes a paid one-on-one sign-up to work with Rice as a personal physician and a free "longevity blueprint" resource, both promotional elements. Anyone with existing joint problems, balance issues, or cardiovascular conditions should get individualized guidance before starting.
