# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice explains that the standard PSA (prostate-specific antigen) test is "prostate-specific," not "cancer-specific": an elevated PSA reflects that something is happening in the prostate — which could be cancer, benign prostate enlargement (BPH), prostatitis, or even recent vigorous exercise — without indicating which.[^new-prostate-cancer-test-is-530-better-than-psa-BWFr0-fZFfo] He states that on its own, an elevated PSA reading corresponds to only about a 30-40% actual likelihood of cancer, and that aggressive prostate cancer doesn't reliably push PSA very high either, creating both false alarms and false reassurance.[^new-prostate-cancer-test-is-530-better-than-psa-BWFr0-fZFfo] He then introduces two newer tests he says close this gap.

# The newer tests

- **Prostate screening epigenetic (PSE) test.** Rather than measuring an indirect protein, this test reads epigenetic markers — chemical tags added to DNA that control gene expression — looking for a pattern of five specific markers he says is distinct to cancerous prostate tissue regardless of gland size, inflammation, or recent activity. He states that combining PSA with PSE correctly identified cancer in 94% of men in early research, describing this as "nearly double" PSA's accuracy alone.[^new-prostate-cancer-test-is-530-better-than-psa-BWFr0-fZFfo]
- **Stockholm 3.** Developed in Sweden, this test combines PSA level with additional protein biomarkers, a panel of genetic risk variants, and clinical factors (age, family history) into a single risk score. He cites a Karolinska Institute study of more than 12,000 men, published in the Annals of Internal Medicine, reporting that Stockholm 3 correctly identified 90% of aggressive prostate cancers versus 74% for PSA alone, while reducing unnecessary biopsies by roughly 45%. He notes Stockholm 3 is already used clinically in parts of Europe and is undergoing U.S. validation.[^new-prostate-cancer-test-is-530-better-than-psa-BWFr0-fZFfo]

As of the video's filming (July 2026), he says neither test is yet widely available or covered by insurance in the U.S., so PSA remains the primary screening tool, but suggests men with an elevated PSA may consider paying out of pocket for PSE or Stockholm 3 alongside it.[^new-prostate-cancer-test-is-530-better-than-psa-BWFr0-fZFfo] He also mentions saw palmetto as having "meaningful evidence" for supporting prostate function and addressing benign enlargement.[^new-prostate-cancer-test-is-530-better-than-psa-BWFr0-fZFfo]

# Caveats

The video's own title claims a 530% accuracy improvement, but the number actually stated in the transcript is 270% (for PSE) and separately a 94%-vs-roughly-48% type comparison is implied for the combined test — the headline figure is not clearly reconciled with the numbers given in the narration, so it should be treated cautiously. The Karolinska/Stockholm 3 study is named with a journal and sample size, but the PSE accuracy claims and the twin-brothers scenario are illustrative rather than sourced to a specific trial. The video includes an affiliate Amazon link for a saw palmetto supplement.
