# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice's central claim is that the standard laboratory cutoffs for blood sugar (fasting glucose 100 mg/dL for prediabetes, 126 for diabetes; A1C 5.7% and 6.5%) were designed only to diagnose diabetes, not to flag the "decade of silent damage" to blood vessels, brain, kidneys, and heart that he says begins at meaningfully lower levels[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4]. He argues that by the time a patient crosses the official diagnostic line, vascular injury has typically been accumulating for years, and that clinicians should instead aim for "optimal" ranges well inside what is currently labeled normal[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].

He supports this with several cited (but not fully referenced) findings: a study of over 10,000 people reportedly linking fasting glucose of 90 to elevated cardiovascular risk versus levels below 85; research associating post-meal glucose spikes above 140-155 with cardiovascular events, cognitive decline, and vessel oxidative damage; and a claim, attributed to the Journal of Neurology, that A1C above 5.5% is linked to accelerated brain aging and dementia risk[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].

# Proposed optimal ranges

- **Fasting glucose**: optimal 75-90 mg/dL; above 90 warrants attention; above 100 (technically prediabetic) is worth acting on[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].
- **Post-meal glucose**: should return below 120 mg/dL within 90 minutes of finishing a meal; spikes above 140 are where he says vascular damage starts[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].
- **Hemoglobin A1C**: optimal 4.7-5.3%; above 5.5% is worth taking seriously even though standard guidelines don't flag concern until 5.7%[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].
- **Bonus marker**: fasting insulin, said to rise years before glucose does, with an optimal level of 6 or below[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].
- He recommends a continuous glucose monitor for about a month to get a fuller individual picture rather than relying on any single number[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].

# Habits to blunt blood sugar without changing food

He also lists four behavior changes he says lower blood sugar independent of diet: eating protein and vegetables before carbohydrates at the same meal (cited as reducing post-meal insulin spikes by up to 57% in a Journal of Diabetes Care study); a 10-minute walk after eating (cited as reducing post-meal spikes up to 30%); protecting sleep (citing a Lancet-published finding that one week of sleep restriction cut insulin sensitivity by up to 30%); and time-restricted eating within a 10-12 hour window (cited to a Journal of Cell Metabolism study)[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4]. He also recommends the supplement berberine as a natural compound for lowering blood sugar and insulin resistance, comparing its effectiveness to metformin[^how-high-is-too-high-for-blood-sugar-vdoTyvFaee4].

# Caveats

The cited studies (fasting-glucose cohort, European Heart Journal, Journal of Neurology, Journal of Diabetes Care, The Lancet, Journal of Cell Metabolism) are named only by journal or loosely described, with no authors, sample sizes, or links given, so their claims cannot be independently verified from the video alone. The video also promotes an affiliate link for a specific berberine supplement, a commercial interest worth noting.
