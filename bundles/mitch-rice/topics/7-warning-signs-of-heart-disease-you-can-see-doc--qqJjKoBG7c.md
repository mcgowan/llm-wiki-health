# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents seven visible or palpable physical signs he says can indicate elevated cardiovascular risk well before a heart attack occurs, arguing that most people, including some doctors, fail to connect these external findings back to heart disease[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c]. He frames each sign as being of limited value on its own but says the presence of two or more warrants prompt medical evaluation[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].

# The seven signs

- Xanthelasma: yellowish, slightly raised patches near the eyelids, linked in a cited European Heart Journal study to higher heart attack risk even when standard cholesterol testing looks only mildly elevated, suggesting it can appear before lab abnormalities show up[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].
- Arcus senilis: a pale ring around the iris from lipid deposits, also associated with elevated cholesterol, though he notes it is common and less concerning on its own past age 60[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].
- Frank's sign: a diagonal earlobe crease correlated in multiple cited studies with coronary artery disease, theorized to reflect small-vessel changes that affect blood flow to the earlobe similarly to the coronary arteries[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].
- Nail clubbing: a spoon-like curving of the fingertip and nail linked to chronically low blood oxygen, associated with certain congenital heart diseases and infective endocarditis[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].
- Splinter hemorrhages: reddish-brown vertical lines under the nail; a single one from trauma is benign, but several appearing spontaneously across multiple fingers can indicate infective endocarditis[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].
- Peripheral edema: leg and ankle swelling from fluid retention, especially symmetric swelling accompanied by shortness of breath while lying flat (orthopnea), can indicate the heart is not pumping efficiently, as in congestive heart failure[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].
- Cyanosis: bluish discoloration of the lips or fingers from low blood oxygenation, potentially reflecting heart failure or a congenital heart defect, treated as an urgent sign especially with sudden onset or accompanying chest discomfort[^7-warning-signs-of-heart-disease-you-can-see-doc--qqJjKoBG7c].
