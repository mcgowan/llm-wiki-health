# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents a 10-minute morning routine intended to reduce dementia risk by addressing three mechanisms he says drive Alzheimer's-type brain damage years before any memory symptoms appear: chronic brain inflammation, reduced neuroplasticity (BDNF signaling), and impaired blood flow to the brain.[^the-10-min-daily-habit-to-prevent-dementia-in-ol-jgOtAao_zNU] He anchors the urgency of starting early on a study he attributes to the Journal of Neurology that followed adults from midlife into their 80s and 90s and found that biological changes such as amyloid buildup, hippocampal shrinkage, and reduced cerebral blood flow begin on average 20 years before the first symptom of dementia appears.[^the-10-min-daily-habit-to-prevent-dementia-in-ol-jgOtAao_zNU]

# The routine

- Minutes 0-2: paced breathing, inhaling through the nose for 4 seconds and exhaling through the mouth for 8 seconds. A longer exhale is said to activate the vagus nerve, which has an anti-inflammatory pathway that can help calm chronic low-grade brain inflammation.[^the-10-min-daily-habit-to-prevent-dementia-in-ol-jgOtAao_zNU]
- Minutes 3-9: "dual-task walking" — walking at a comfortable pace for about 7 minutes while simultaneously performing a mental task, such as counting backward from 100 by sevens, naming items in a category with each step, alternating letters and numbers, or reciting something from memory. Rice says combining a physical and cognitive task forces different brain regions to coordinate, producing a larger release of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) than walking alone, and cites a study he attributes to the Annals of Internal Medicine reporting that this "dual-task" walking outperformed regular walking for brain-health outcomes and was linked to reduced dementia risk over follow-up.[^the-10-min-daily-habit-to-prevent-dementia-in-ol-jgOtAao_zNU]
- The final minute: humming, said to dramatically increase nasal nitric oxide production — up to 15-fold versus quiet breathing, per a study he attributes to the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine — which is claimed to relax and open blood vessels feeding the brain and improve cerebral blood flow.[^the-10-min-daily-habit-to-prevent-dementia-in-ol-jgOtAao_zNU]

# Caveats

The named journals (Annals of Internal Medicine, American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine, Journal of Neurology) are not accompanied by specific study titles, authors, or sample sizes, so the cited findings cannot be verified as described. The video also promotes a paid, waitlisted private community as a further product offering alongside the free routine.
