# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice describes ten foods he says he personally eats every week, framing each around a specific nutrient or mechanism tied to blood pressure and cardiovascular health rather than general dietary advice[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA]. His overall argument is that a heart-healthy diet does not need to be restrictive or exclude foods often labeled unhealthy — such as eggs, steak, and full-fat yogurt — as long as the choices support stable blood sugar, muscle mass, and artery function[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].

# The ten foods

- **Eggs** (2–4 daily): a complete protein with choline, vitamin D, B12, and selenium; he argues dietary cholesterol from eggs has little cardiovascular impact compared with the blood-sugar stability a protein-based breakfast provides[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Steak** (weekly): valued for preserving lean muscle mass, which he describes as a "glucose storage tank" that improves insulin sensitivity and lowers inflammation; also a source of creatine, zinc, B12, and iron[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Salmon** (twice weekly): wild-caught salmon supplies EPA/DHA omega-3s said to reduce arterial inflammation and improve vessel flexibility, countering age-related artery stiffness[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Beans and lentils** (five times weekly): provide potassium, magnesium, fiber, and protein — potassium to relax artery walls, magnesium to calm the nervous system, and fiber to feed gut bacteria linked to lower cardiovascular inflammation[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Cooked tomatoes**: cooking is said to release more lycopene, an antioxidant claimed to protect LDL cholesterol from oxidation and reduce (or even reverse) arterial plaque[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Blueberries** (five times weekly): flavonoids and anthocyanins are said to improve arterial lining responsiveness and lower oxidative stress; he cites a study finding daily blueberry intake over eight weeks produced blood-pressure drops "comparable to some medications"[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Chicken**: another muscle-preserving protein source, framed the same way as steak[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Avocado** (about half, most days): roughly twice the potassium of a banana by weight, plus monounsaturated fat that he says improves absorption of fat-soluble nutrients, including tomato lycopene[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Plain Greek yogurt**: high protein and probiotics without spiking blood sugar; fermented-dairy bacteria are said to lower inflammation and sympathetic nervous system tone[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].
- **Sardines**: an affordable, low-mercury, shelf-stable omega-3 source comparable to salmon, also supplying calcium, vitamin D, B12, and selenium[^10-healthiest-foods-i-eat-every-week-as-a-doctor-RfPPZnGXtNA].

# Caveats

This is one physician's personal, non-systematic food list, not a clinical dietary guideline, and most mechanistic claims (e.g., lycopene "reversing" plaque, blueberries matching medication-level blood-pressure reductions) are stated without naming the underlying studies, sample sizes, or effect sizes. The video is sponsored — it promotes a specific air fryer brand with a discount code and affiliate/Amazon links — which is a commercial interest to note alongside the food recommendations.
