# Overview

Primary care physician Dr. Mitch Rice compares magnesium and potassium as blood-pressure-lowering minerals, stating that both help but through different mechanisms.[^magnesium-vs-potassium-which-one-is-best-for-blo-FOp05vSbuhc] Potassium's primary action is prompting the kidneys to excrete excess sodium — and with it, retained water — while also mildly relaxing vessel walls; magnesium's primary action is relaxing the smooth muscle in artery walls and quieting the stress (sympathetic) response that otherwise tightens vessels and raises heart rate.[^magnesium-vs-potassium-which-one-is-best-for-blo-FOp05vSbuhc] He frames the practical question as which mineral fits a given person's underlying driver of high blood pressure rather than which mineral is objectively superior.

# Potassium

Most adults, especially those over 50, fall short of recommended potassium intake (roughly 3,400 mg/day for men, 2,600 mg/day for women) because modern diets run high in sodium and low in whole foods such as avocados, spinach, beans, lentils, yogurt, salmon, and potatoes.[^magnesium-vs-potassium-which-one-is-best-for-blo-FOp05vSbuhc] He cites study data suggesting higher potassium intake lowers systolic blood pressure by roughly 4-5 points on average, with a larger effect in people eating high-sodium diets.[^magnesium-vs-potassium-which-one-is-best-for-blo-FOp05vSbuhc] He flags an important safety caveat: people over 60, those with chronic kidney disease or reduced kidney function, or those on ACE inhibitors or ARBs should not take high-dose potassium supplements without medical supervision, since impaired potassium clearance can raise blood potassium to levels that trigger dangerous heart rhythms (V-tach/V-fib).[^magnesium-vs-potassium-which-one-is-best-for-blo-FOp05vSbuhc]

# Magnesium

Magnesium is described as working through relaxation rather than fluid balance — relaxing arterial smooth muscle and calming the nervous system's stress response, which he says also improves sleep and blood sugar handling. He cites a comparable effect size to potassium, roughly a 5-point average reduction in systolic pressure, though he notes some data show a larger effect.[^magnesium-vs-potassium-which-one-is-best-for-blo-FOp05vSbuhc] In his clinical experience, magnesium tends to help most with morning blood-pressure spikes, stress-related spikes, poor sleep, and muscle tension. He recommends magnesium glycinate specifically for better gastrointestinal tolerance, absorption, and sleep benefit over cheaper forms like oxide or citrate, at typical doses of 240-480 mg (up to about 600 mg), while cautioning that higher doses can cause loose stools, which can itself worsen blood pressure through dehydration.[^magnesium-vs-potassium-which-one-is-best-for-blo-FOp05vSbuhc]

# Caveats

Neither mineral is presented as clearly superior, and the video ends by noting that potassium regulation itself depends on adequate magnesium, and that lifestyle factors (exercise, diet, breathwork, sleep) matter more than supplement choice. The specific effect-size figures (4-5 points, 5 points) are stated without naming the underlying studies, sample sizes, or populations, and the video includes affiliate Amazon links for both a magnesium glycinate product and a potassium supplement, plus a plug for the creator's paid live workshop.
