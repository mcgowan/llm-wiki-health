# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents five physical signs he says can be visually self-checked and may indicate elevated blood pressure, each tied to a specific mechanism of vascular strain.[^5-signs-of-high-blood-pressure-you-can-actually-OSE1dXlU_qo] His overall framing is that no single sign is diagnostic on its own, but that two or more appearing together and persisting over weeks to months — particularly in adults over 60 — form a meaningful early-warning pattern worth bringing to a doctor.[^5-signs-of-high-blood-pressure-you-can-actually-OSE1dXlU_qo]

# The five signs

- **Facial flushing**: small facial blood vessels dilate rapidly when blood pressure spikes, causing visible redness with stress, caffeine, heat, or exertion; more concerning when paired with recurring headaches.[^5-signs-of-high-blood-pressure-you-can-actually-OSE1dXlU_qo]
- **Bloodshot eyes / broken vessels**: persistent redness or visibly broken vessels in the eye whites, especially without an obvious cause like rubbing or allergies, is linked to hypertensive damage; sustained high blood pressure can also stretch and rupture the retina's small vessels (hypertensive retinopathy), sometimes detectable by an eye exam alone.[^5-signs-of-high-blood-pressure-you-can-actually-OSE1dXlU_qo]
- **Visible arterial pulsing** (temples, neck): stiffened, less elastic arteries transmit the pulse wave outward instead of absorbing it, producing a noticeable throb at rest — described as one of the most overlooked but easiest signs to spot.[^5-signs-of-high-blood-pressure-you-can-actually-OSE1dXlU_qo]
- **Recurrent nosebleeds**: repeated (e.g., weekly over weeks to months) nosebleeds, especially combined with flushing, headache, or pulsing, are attributed to rupture of fragile small vessels inside the nose under elevated pressure; occasional nosebleeds from dry air or irritation are not considered a sign on their own.[^5-signs-of-high-blood-pressure-you-can-actually-OSE1dXlU_qo]
- **Ankle/leg swelling (peripheral edema)**: stiffened vessels impede blood return to the heart while kidney vessel damage from high blood pressure promotes sodium and water retention, with gravity pulling excess fluid into the lower legs, worse at night. A self-check for pitting edema is pressing a thumb into the shin/ankle for 5 seconds — if the resulting dent slowly fills back in, that's a sign of possible heart or kidney strain.[^5-signs-of-high-blood-pressure-you-can-actually-OSE1dXlU_qo]
