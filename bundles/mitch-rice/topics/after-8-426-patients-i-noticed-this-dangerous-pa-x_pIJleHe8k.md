# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that LDL cholesterol alone is a weak, context-free predictor of heart disease risk, and that statins — while genuinely beneficial for people with established heart disease or multiple major risk factors — may be overprescribed to metabolically healthy people whose high LDL does not carry the same risk[^after-8-426-patients-i-noticed-this-dangerous-pa-x_pIJleHe8k]. His central concept is that LDL particles are only dangerous when traveling through an inflamed, insulin-resistant "environment," a framing he uses to explain why some lean, insulin-sensitive people with very high LDL ("lean mass hyper-responders") may not face the same cardiovascular risk as metabolically unhealthy people with the same LDL number[^after-8-426-patients-i-noticed-this-dangerous-pa-x_pIJleHe8k].

# Statin side effects discussed

He lists four side effects he says are underdiscussed: muscle and joint pain (cited at 10-25% of users, rarely progressing to rhabdomyolysis); increased type 2 diabetes risk via impaired pancreatic insulin secretion and reduced muscle insulin sensitivity, citing a Lancet meta-analysis; CoQ10 depletion from statins blocking the HMG-CoA reductase pathway, which he says can be partly offset with CoQ10 supplementation; and reversible cognitive decline, referencing the FDA's 2012 label warning about memory loss and confusion in some users[^after-8-426-patients-i-noticed-this-dangerous-pa-x_pIJleHe8k].

# The "heart panel" alternative

Instead of relying on LDL alone, Rice recommends six markers: apoB (particle count, a stronger risk predictor than LDL mass), lipoprotein(a) (a largely genetic risk factor he says should be tested at least once in a lifetime), the triglyceride-to-HDL ratio (with targets of triglycerides under 100 and HDL above 60), fasting insulin and blood sugar, hs-CRP (inflammation), and a coronary artery calcium score (direct imaging of arterial plaque)[^after-8-426-patients-i-noticed-this-dangerous-pa-x_pIJleHe8k].

# Caveats

The "8,426 patients" figure in the title reflects Rice's self-described clinical experience rather than a formal study or published dataset; the cited Lancet meta-analysis and other referenced research lack full details (authors, year, sample size); and the "lean mass hyper-responder" framing, while an active area of research discussion, is presented without citing specific supporting trials[^after-8-426-patients-i-noticed-this-dangerous-pa-x_pIJleHe8k].
