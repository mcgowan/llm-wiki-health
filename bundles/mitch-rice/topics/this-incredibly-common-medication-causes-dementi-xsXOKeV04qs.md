# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that a class of drugs called anticholinergics — found in many everyday prescription and over-the-counter products — blocks the neurotransmitter acetylcholine and, with regular long-term use, is associated with meaningfully higher dementia risk[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs]. He frames acetylcholine as central to memory formation and neuron-to-neuron communication, such that chronically blunting it degrades cognition over time and, he says, is linked in brain-imaging research to measurable hippocampal shrinkage and reduced brain metabolism rather than just transient fogginess[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs]. He cites a meta-analysis of roughly 1.5 million people identifying long-term anticholinergic use as an independent risk factor for Alzheimer's disease and all-cause dementia, and a separate pooled analysis of 21 trials in which about three months of regular use raised dementia risk by an average of 46-50%[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].

# Medication categories and alternatives

Rice walks through six everyday drug categories he considers anticholinergic offenders, pairing each with a suggested substitute:

- **Older antihistamines** (diphenhydramine/Benadryl, ZzzQuil) — he calls this the biggest and most surprising offender since it crosses the blood-brain barrier readily; he suggests second-generation antihistamines (Claritin, Zyrtec, Allegra, rotated every three months) or nasal steroids/saline rinses instead[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].
- **OTC sleep aids** (ZzzQuil, Tylenol PM, Unisom) — same diphenhydramine mechanism nightly; alternatives suggested are magnesium glycinate, ashwagandha, or prescription trazodone[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].
- **Anxiety medication** (hydroxyzine/Vistaril) — flagged as a strong anticholinergic frequently prescribed to adults over 60, a group already experiencing natural acetylcholine decline; SSRIs/SNRIs or therapy are proposed instead[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].
- **Bladder control drugs** (Ditropan, Vesicare) — work by blocking acetylcholine in bladder smooth muscle but reach the brain too; Myrbetriq is offered as a non-anticholinergic alternative[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].
- **IBS/gut-spasm medication** (Bentyl) — alternatives suggested are peppermint oil capsules and a low-FODMAP diet[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].
- **Motion-sickness drugs** (scopolamine, Dramamine) — potent even short-term; acupressure wristbands and ginger supplements are suggested for travel[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].

He emphasizes that effects are cumulative and synergistic: stacking two or three of these drugs (e.g., hydroxyzine plus ZzzQuil plus Bentyl) compounds acetylcholine blockade rather than simply adding it, and he advises reviewing every medication in one's cabinet with a doctor or pharmacist, especially for adults over 60, those with existing memory issues, multiple anticholinergic prescriptions, or chronic conditions like hypertension, diabetes, or poor sleep[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs]. He cautions against stopping any prescription abruptly without medical guidance[^this-incredibly-common-medication-causes-dementi-xsXOKeV04qs].
