# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice presents a 5-minute daily routine designed to lower blood pressure by calming the autonomic nervous system, and pairs it with a specific protocol for taking home blood pressure readings so that anxiety about the measurement itself does not distort the numbers.[^the-5-min-daily-habit-to-lower-blood-pressure-in-8WqxRbdl5aU] His core argument is that sustained "fight-or-flight" activation keeps arteries tighter and heart rate higher throughout the day, and that a short daily practice of shifting into a calmer state can meaningfully lower readings, particularly in adults over 60.[^the-5-min-daily-habit-to-lower-blood-pressure-in-8WqxRbdl5aU]

# The 5-minute routine

- Minutes 0-2: seated breathing — inhale through the nose for 5 seconds, exhale through the mouth for 5 seconds — said to shift the body toward "rest-and-digest" parasympathetic activity, with a reported 5-10 point drop in some patients.[^the-5-min-daily-habit-to-lower-blood-pressure-in-8WqxRbdl5aU]
- Minutes 2-4: a brief progressive-muscle-relaxation sequence — raising and slowly lowering the shoulders, squeezing the fists for 5 seconds and releasing, and squeezing the thighs for 5 seconds and releasing, repeated for 2 minutes — on the reasoning that releasing muscle tension sends calming signals back to the brain that help arteries stay open.[^the-5-min-daily-habit-to-lower-blood-pressure-in-8WqxRbdl5aU]
- Minute 4-5: one minute of sitting still with eyes closed and no stimulation, meant to let the calming signal "lock in" before the routine ends.[^the-5-min-daily-habit-to-lower-blood-pressure-in-8WqxRbdl5aU]

# Using it to get more accurate readings

Rice recommends doing this routine immediately before checking blood pressure at home, arguing that checking without first calming down often measures anticipation and muscle tension rather than a true resting value, producing false-high readings, unnecessary panic, and even unwarranted medication changes. He also advises taking two readings one minute apart, recording the lower one, and tracking the average over many days rather than reacting to any single reading.[^the-5-min-daily-habit-to-lower-blood-pressure-in-8WqxRbdl5aU]

# Caveats

The reported 5-10 point drop and related claims are based on Rice's own clinical impression ("many of my own patients") rather than a cited study, so they should be read as anecdotal. The video also promotes the creator's own paid live "blood pressure reset workshop."
