# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that people who eat a healthy diet can still have high cholesterol because diet is only a minor input into circulating cholesterol levels: he states the liver produces roughly 80% of the body's cholesterol, with food accounting for only about 20%, so even a very clean diet cannot override a liver that is genetically set to overproduce.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-cholesterol-d-DBQqEo_5r8E] He lays out three "upstream" mechanisms he says are overlooked in typical cholesterol conversations: excess liver production driven by refined carbohydrate and sugar intake, a gut microbiome that recycles bile acids instead of eliminating them, and subclinical thyroid dysfunction that slows cholesterol clearance.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-cholesterol-d-DBQqEo_5r8E]

# Three proposed drivers

On liver production, he explains that refined carbohydrates and sugar spike insulin, which activates the enzyme HMG-CoA reductase — the same enzyme statin drugs block — prompting the liver to manufacture more cholesterol; he cites a study in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition as showing that replacing dietary fat with refined carbohydrates increases the liver's VLDL output, which converts into LDL.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-cholesterol-d-DBQqEo_5r8E] On gut health, he describes bile acids made from cholesterol that are normally excreted in stool, but says certain gut bacteria convert bile acids into a form that is reabsorbed instead, recycling cholesterol back into circulation; he cites a study in Cell Host & Microbe linking lower microbiome diversity to impaired bile acid elimination and higher LDL.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-cholesterol-d-DBQqEo_5r8E] On thyroid function, he says even mild, "subclinical" hypothyroidism (a slightly elevated TSH with normal free T4) can slow the liver's cholesterol clearance, a pattern he says is common and easily missed in adults over 50; he cites a study in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism reporting that treating subclinical hypothyroidism lowered LDL without dietary change.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-cholesterol-d-DBQqEo_5r8E]

# What to ask your doctor

He recommends requesting a full thyroid panel (TSH plus free T4), a fasting insulin level, and a conversation about whether diet supports bile acid elimination, rather than defaulting to further fat restriction.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-cholesterol-d-DBQqEo_5r8E] He also points to the 2020 Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee's scientific report as evidence that the link between dietary saturated fat and cardiovascular events is weaker than long assumed, particularly in people who are otherwise metabolically healthy.[^why-healthy-people-still-have-high-cholesterol-d-DBQqEo_5r8E]

# Caveats

The three cited journal studies (American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, Cell Host & Microbe, Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism) are named only in passing with no authors, publication years, or sample sizes given, so they cannot be independently verified from this video alone. The video closes with a pitch for a paid private community/waitlist covering cholesterol and metabolic health, which is a direct commercial interest in this exact topic.
