# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that some of his most diligent, health-conscious patients have stubbornly high blood pressure precisely because they check it too often, triggering an anticipatory stress response each time — a "checking spiral" that he compares to white-coat hypertension occurring at home instead of in a clinic.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-to-you-here-ME53hg0U0Ls] He explains that the moment someone reaches for the cuff, the nervous system can pre-emptively raise heart rate and constrict arteries in anticipation of a bad number, so the reading captures anticipatory stress rather than true resting blood pressure; checking five, eight, or ten times a day repeats this small cardiovascular event and can make each subsequent reading worse.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-to-you-here-ME53hg0U0Ls]

# The checking spiral and the fix

Because blood pressure naturally fluctuates dozens of times a day with posture, digestion, hydration, and emotion, he argues a single elevated reading is often just normal variation misread as an emergency, prompting repeated re-checks that compound anxiety and raise numbers further.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-to-you-here-ME53hg0U0Ls] His recommended protocol: check only twice daily (morning before coffee/medication, and evening before bed); sit quietly with back and feet supported for a full five minutes beforehand with no phone or conversation; take two readings one minute apart and record the lower one; and track the seven-day average rather than reacting to individual numbers.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-to-you-here-ME53hg0U0Ls] He also recommends a brief breathing exercise (5 seconds in through the nose, 5 seconds out through the mouth, for 3–5 minutes) before each reading to engage the parasympathetic nervous system and reduce the anticipatory spike.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-to-you-here-ME53hg0U0Ls]

# Mindset and cognitive reframing

He frames the core fix as psychological as much as procedural: treating blood pressure as a fluctuating physiological measurement rather than a "report card" on one's health or effort, so that a single number above 130 does not trigger a stress response that itself elevates blood pressure.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-to-you-here-ME53hg0U0Ls] For patients with health-related anxiety specifically, he suggests magnesium glycinate at night to support calmer sleep, which he says can also help blood pressure.[^your-blood-pressure-monitor-is-lying-to-you-here-ME53hg0U0Ls]

# Caveats

The claim that anxiety about readings raises blood pressure "more than actual hypertension" in some patients is presented as personal clinical observation ("dozens of people that I know") rather than data from a study. The video includes an Amazon affiliate link for a specific magnesium glycinate supplement and promotes a free lead-magnet guide ("Blood Pressure Blueprint").
