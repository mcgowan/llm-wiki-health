# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that how well someone ages is better predicted by five trainable physical capabilities than by genetics or calendar age alone, drawing on his primary-care experience of patients whose biological age diverges sharply from their chronological age[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8]. His broader claim, citing epigenetic-clock research, is that improving these five capabilities at any age can measurably lower a person's biological age relative to their birth-certificate age, and that decline in these areas can likewise age the body faster than expected[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8].

# The five capabilities

- **Rising from the floor without hands, knees, or pushing off anything.** Rice presents this as a combined test of leg strength, hip mobility, core stability, balance, and coordination, citing a study in the *European Journal of Preventive Cardiology* that followed over 2,000 adults for a decade and found needing both hands and knees to rise was linked to more than five times the mortality risk. The suggested fix is two to three weekly resistance sessions plus daily hip-mobility work[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8].
- **Balancing on one leg with eyes closed for 10+ seconds**, said to reflect the integration of joints, muscles, vision, and brain that governs fall risk; falls after 65 are described as a leading cause of injury death and loss of independence. The suggested practice is standing on alternating legs daily (e.g., while brushing teeth), progressing from eyes open to eyes closed[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8].
- **Walking briskly for 10 minutes without getting winded**, framed as a marker of cardiorespiratory fitness linked to lower heart attack, stroke, and mortality risk; the recommended target is about 150 minutes per week of "zone two" aerobic activity where conversation is possible but strained[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8].
- **Grip strength**, cited as a proxy for total-body muscle mass, insulin sensitivity, and systemic resilience. Rice references a *Lancet* study of over 140,000 adults across 17 countries finding grip strength predicted cardiovascular death better than systolic blood pressure, and gives benchmarks of roughly 26 kg for men and 16 kg for women, testable with a hand dynamometer or a rolled towel squeeze[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8].
- **Fast heart-rate and breathing recovery after exertion** (within 1-2 minutes), said to reflect parasympathetic nervous system tone; slower recovery is linked to higher cardiovascular and mortality risk. The suggested fix is consistent aerobic activity, plus omega-3/fish oil supplementation, which he says is well-researched for heart-rate variability and arterial stiffness[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8].

# Practical approach

Rice recommends identifying the single weakest of the five capabilities and working on it specifically for about 90 days before moving to the next, rather than trying to improve all five simultaneously[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8]. He closes by citing a study in the journal *Cell* using epigenetic aging clocks across thousands of adults, reporting that people who scored well on capabilities like these had biological ages 5-15 years younger than their chronological age, while poor scorers skewed biologically older[^at-70-if-your-body-can-still-do-these-5-things-y-tIHoLBmBgr8].

# Caveats

The named studies (European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, The Lancet, Cell) are described only in passing without full citations, so their exact methodology and applicability can't be verified from the video alone. The video includes an affiliate Amazon link for a specific omega-3/fish-oil product and promotes a free "metabolic blueprint" guide, both commercial incentives layered onto the clinical advice.
