# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice describes repeated post-meal blood sugar "spikes and crashes" — not full diabetes, just a gradual upward drift in blood sugar seen in many non-diabetic patients — as an underappreciated driver of accelerated aging, weight gain, and inflammation[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY]. He argues that common advice like cutting calories, skipping meals, or eating low-fat does not address the actual mechanism, and instead offers five strategies, two of which involve no dietary change at all, aimed at slowing how fast glucose enters the bloodstream or clearing it faster once it does[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY].

# The five strategies

- **Meal sequencing**: eating protein and vegetables before carbohydrates at the same meal slows gastric emptying and glucose absorption; he cites research in Diabetes Care reporting up to a 37% reduction in post-meal blood sugar spikes and up to 57% lower insulin spikes from this ordering alone, with identical foods and calories[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY].
- **Pre-meal vinegar**: one tablespoon of apple cider vinegar diluted in 10-16 oz of water, taken 10-15 minutes before eating, is said to slow gastric emptying and inhibit starch-digesting enzymes; he cites a European Journal of Clinical Nutrition study reporting up to a 34% reduction in post-meal blood sugar with vinegar before a high-carb meal, noting plain white vinegar works similarly but has less supporting data[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY].
- **Post-meal walk**: a 10-minute walk within 30 minutes of finishing a meal lets contracting leg muscles pull glucose from the blood without needing insulin; he states this can reduce blood sugar spikes by up to 30%, with the effect strongest the sooner the walk starts after eating[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY].
- **Food pairing**: never eating carbohydrates alone — pairing fruit with nuts or yogurt, bread with eggs or avocado, rice or pasta with protein and vegetables — to slow glucose absorption whenever carbs are eaten as snacks, not just at full meals[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY].
- **Sleep and cortisol management**: citing a Lancet finding that one week of restricted sleep cut insulin sensitivity by up to 30% in healthy adults, he recommends 7-8 hours of sleep, avoiding the phone immediately upon waking, and five minutes of slow nasal breathing before breakfast to blunt morning cortisol, which independently raises blood sugar by prompting the liver to release stored glucose[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY].

# Suggested starting point

He recommends picking just one change to start: eating protein before carbs at the next meal, taking a 10-minute walk after dinner, never eating a carbohydrate alone again, trying vinegar before the day's highest-carb meal, or getting to bed 30 minutes earlier[^turn-off-blood-sugar-spikes-in-minutes-without-c-EIVz-UKYBlY].
