# Overview

Dr. Mitch Rice argues that standard multivitamins are largely ineffective because they dose nutrients at levels meant only to prevent outright deficiency rather than optimize health, and because packing 20-plus nutrients into a single tablet creates competition for absorption[^87-of-multivitamins-are-useless-take-this-instea-ISxxDszv3AE]. He cites a meta-analysis in the Annals of Internal Medicine and a separate JAMA analysis, both reportedly finding no meaningful reduction in cardiovascular disease, cancer, cognitive decline, or all-cause mortality from multivitamin use in generally healthy adults, and recommends targeted, higher-dose single supplements instead[^87-of-multivitamins-are-useless-take-this-instea-ISxxDszv3AE].

# The dosing and competition problems

He notes several specific absorption conflicts: calcium and magnesium compete for the same intestinal absorption pathway; iron suppresses zinc absorption when taken at the same meal; and fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K require dietary fat to absorb, which a tablet taken with water on an empty stomach undermines[^87-of-multivitamins-are-useless-take-this-instea-ISxxDszv3AE]. He also flags that multivitamins typically supply only 400-800 IU of vitamin D3, versus a cited therapeutic range of 2,000-5,000 IU, and 50-100 mg of magnesium against a stated adult need where over 75% of adults are said to be deficient[^87-of-multivitamins-are-useless-take-this-instea-ISxxDszv3AE].

# Recommended targeted supplements

Rice's two primary recommendations are magnesium glycinate (or magnesium threonate for cognitive concerns), at 200-400 mg of elemental magnesium daily, said to support blood pressure, sleep, insulin sensitivity, and inflammation; and vitamin D3 (2,000-5,000 IU daily) combined with vitamin K2/MK-7 (100-200 mcg), with D3 said to affect over a thousand genes and K2 needed to direct absorbed calcium into bone rather than arteries and soft tissue[^87-of-multivitamins-are-useless-take-this-instea-ISxxDszv3AE].

# Caveats

The cited Annals of Internal Medicine and JAMA analyses are not identified by author, year, or sample size; the video promotes Rice's own affiliate-linked magnesium and vitamin D3/K2 supplement products as well as a broader list of "targeted supplements" available through his description links[^87-of-multivitamins-are-useless-take-this-instea-ISxxDszv3AE].
