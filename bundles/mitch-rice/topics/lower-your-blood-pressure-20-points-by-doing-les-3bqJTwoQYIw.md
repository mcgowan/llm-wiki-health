# Overview

Family medicine physician Dr. Mitch Rice argues that people trying to lower blood pressure often work harder than necessary — cutting all salt, exercising daily, and obsessing over every reading — while seeing little improvement.[^lower-your-blood-pressure-20-points-by-doing-les-3bqJTwoQYIw] His alternative framing is that blood pressure is not a fixed number but a value that naturally swings 20-30 points across a normal day in response to activity, meals, and stress, so the goal is to work with that variability rather than fight it.[^lower-your-blood-pressure-20-points-by-doing-les-3bqJTwoQYIw]

He presents three "levers" he says make the process easier: measurement habits, insulin control through food pairing, and a short daily breathing practice to calm the nervous system.[^lower-your-blood-pressure-20-points-by-doing-les-3bqJTwoQYIw]

# The three levers

- **Fix how you measure.** Checking once at a random, stressful moment (or checking repeatedly throughout the day) can create a feedback loop where seeing an unwanted number triggers a stress response that raises the next reading. The recommended fix is to check at the same times daily — once before coffee in the morning and once before bed — sitting quietly for 5 minutes beforehand, taking two readings a minute apart and recording the lower one, then using the 7-day average as the number to act on rather than any single reading.[^lower-your-blood-pressure-20-points-by-doing-les-3bqJTwoQYIw]
- **Lower insulin to lower blood pressure.** Refined carbohydrates eaten alone (white bread, sugary drinks, pastries) spike blood sugar and insulin; he claims high insulin causes the kidneys to retain sodium and causes blood vessels to constrict, both of which raise pressure. The suggested fix is to always pair carbohydrates with protein or fat, avoid sweet drinks on an empty stomach, and include protein at the first meal of the day.[^lower-your-blood-pressure-20-points-by-doing-les-3bqJTwoQYIw]
- **Calm the nervous system.** Because the body's "fight or flight" and "rest and digest" states can't coexist, and low-grade daily stressors keep many people in the former, he recommends a daily 5-minute practice: sitting quietly and breathing in through the nose for 5 seconds and out through the mouth for 5 seconds. He cites this pattern of slow breathing as capable of lowering systolic pressure by 5-10 points in a single session, with repeated practice making the effect more lasting.[^lower-your-blood-pressure-20-points-by-doing-les-3bqJTwoQYIw]

# Caveats

This is a single physician's own framework presented in a short YouTube video, not a peer-reviewed protocol; the claimed figures (20-30 point daily swings, 5-10 point drops from breathing) are stated without citing specific studies, sample sizes, or methodology. The video promotes the creator's own free "Blood Pressure Blueprint" guide and links to other videos on his channel, which is a promotional element worth noting when weighing the advice.
